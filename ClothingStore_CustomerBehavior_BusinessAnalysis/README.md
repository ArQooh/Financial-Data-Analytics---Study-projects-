#  Clothing Store Customer Behavior Analysis  
**Data & Business Recommendations Report | United States Market**

---

##  **Project Overview**

This analytical project examines customer behavior, purchasing patterns, profitability drivers, and loyalty metrics of a clothing retail store operating in the United States.  

The analysis identifies key customer segments, product performance, shipping preferences, subscription effectiveness, geographic strengths, and seasonal trends. Based on these insights, clear and actionable recommendations are provided to improve revenue, customer retention, and operational efficiency.

##  **Key Findings**

### 1. Gender Distribution & Revenue Contribution
- Male customers form the largest segment and **outnumber female customers by a factor of approximately 2**.
- Female customers represent only **~32%** of the total customer base.
- The female segment currently does **not use the subscription service** at all.

### 2. Discount Impact on Orders and Revenue
- There are **32% more orders without a discount** than orders with a discount.
- Revenue from non-discounted sales is **34% higher** than revenue from discounted sales.
- Non-subscribers apply discounts almost **twice less frequently** than subscribers, while maintaining the same average order value.

### 4. Shipping Method Performance
- Average order value is higher for **Express shipping**.
- However, the overall difference in total contribution between Express and Standard shipping is **less than 5%**.

### 5. Subscription Effectiveness
- Non-subscribers account for **74%** of all customers and generate **73%** of total profit.
- Average order value is identical between subscribers and non-subscribers.
- Repeat (loyal) customers **without** a subscription outnumber repeat customers **with** a subscription by nearly **2.5×**.
- Conclusion: The current subscription model is **not delivering the expected benefits** and requires significant revision.

### 6. Age Group Performance
| Age Group       | Share of Customers | Share of Total Profit |
|-----------------|--------------------|-----------------------|
| 30–59 (Middle)  | **55%**            | **54%**               |
| 18–29 (Young)   | **26%**            | **26%**                     |
| 60+ (Seniors)   | **19%**            | **20%**                     |

- The average customer age is approximately **44 years**.
- The 18–29 segment significantly underperforms relative to the dominant middle-aged group.

### 7. Category Performance
- The **Clothing** category is the clear profit leader, contributing **44%** of total profit.
- **Outerwear** and **Footwear** categories are lagging and underperforming.

### 8. Payment, Delivery & Purchase Frequency Preferences
- The most common combination preferred by customers is **PayPal + Express delivery** (17.36% of customers).
- The majority of customers make purchases **every three months** or **annually**.

### 9. Geographic Insights
- **Montana** is the most profitable state in terms of both sales volume and number of customers, and it also records the highest rate of positive reviews.
- **Illinois** has the highest number of loyal (repeat) customers. 

This indicates strong customer loyalty and makes the state particularly important from a customer-retention perspective.
- Strong performance is also observed in **California, Nevada, Idaho**, and neighboring Western states. 

These markets should receive particular attention when considering customer retention and business expansion.

At the same time, the company should investigate opportunities to expand its presence in Central and Eastern U.S. markets where performance is comparatively weaker.
- **Kansas** shows the weakest results in both profit and customer base. 

This suggests that Kansas requires additional investigation before further resources are allocated.
- Purchase activity peaks during **Spring** and **Autumn**.

This suggests that seasonal demand should play an important role in inventory planning and marketing campaigns.

The company should increase inventory availability before peak seasons and prepare seasonal marketing campaigns in advance.

##  **Recommendations**

### High-Priority Actions

| Priority | Recommendation | Rationale |
|----------|----------------|-----------|
| 1 | **Expand the female customer segment** through targeted marketing and product assortment | Women represent only ~32% of customers and currently do not engage with the subscription program. The female segment also demonstrates strong product ratings, indicating that product acceptance may provide a foundation for further growth. |
| 2 | **Revise the subscription model**, with special focus on making it attractive to female customers | The subscription program currently does not demonstrate a clear financial advantage. Non-subscribers generate approximately 73% of total profit and account for approximately 74% of customers, while repeat customers without subscriptions significantly outnumber subscribed repeat customers. Subscription is used almost exclusively by male customers (~70% of the base) and fails to increase average order value or loyalty. |
| 3 | **Strengthen focus on high-performing Western states** (Montana, California, Illinois, Nevada, Idaho and neighboring states) | These regions deliver the best combination of profit, customer volume, and loyalty. |
| 4 | **Strengthen High-Performing Categoriees** | Clothing generates approximately 44% of total profit. |
| 5 | **Prioritize Express shipping** in promotions and operations | Higher average order value with only a marginal difference in total contribution. |

### Medium-Priority Actions

| Priority | Recommendation | Rationale |
|----------|----------------|-----------|
| 6 | Expand Clothing category offerings specifically for the **18–29 age group** | This segment is under-indexed relative to the dominant 30–59 group. |
| 7 | Develop and promote products suitable for **more frequent purchasing cycles** (weekly / monthly) | Most customers currently buy only quarterly or annually — opportunity to increase purchase frequency. |
| 8 | Improve and expand the **Outerwear** category for the winter season | Outerwear is currently lagging behind the strong Clothing category. |
| 9 | Increase marketing and assortment presence in **Central and Eastern states**, starting with underperforming markets such as Kansas | Geographic diversification opportunity while protecting the strong Western core. |
| 10 | Maintain and further optimize the top-rated products for both genders (especially high-revenue men’s items) | These products already demonstrate strong rating and revenue performance. |

---

## **Summary of Strategic Focus Areas**

**Should do:**
- Expand the **female segment**
- Improve and redesign the **subscription program** (especially for women)
- Concentrate resources on **Western high-performing states**
- Prioritize **Express shipping**
- Expand **Clothing** offerings for young adults (18–29)
- Focus product and marketing efforts on **Spring & Autumn**
- Develop more frequent-purchase product lines
- Strengthen **Outerwear** for the winter season
- Diversify into Central and Eastern regions while protecting the Western core

---

##  **Conclusion**

The business currently generates the majority of its profit from middle-aged male customers, the Clothing category, non-discounted sales, and strong Western states (led by Montana).  

However, significant untapped potential exists in:
- The female customer segment  
- Younger customers (18–29)  
- A redesigned subscription model  
- Underperforming categories (Outerwear, Footwear)  
- Geographic expansion beyond the Western core  

By reallocating resources toward the high-ROI segments and products identified above, while systematically addressing the underperforming areas, the company can improve both short-term profitability and long-term customer lifetime value.
